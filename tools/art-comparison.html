Original unit language vs our new roster

My assessment: our new set is more disciplined and less cartoonish than our earlier remake. The original still has stronger chunky silhouettes, value contrast and science-fiction identity. Its abstraction is purposeful; realism alone does not improve a tactical icon.

Research review · 2026-09-20. Remake icons remain 32 × 32 with no enlargement. External reference images retain their published dimensions; the archive chart is not a verified native sprite atlas. No game artwork was changed for this comparison.

Where our interpretation departs from the original

  • Aircraft: our Falcon is a familiar delta-wing jet. The reference designs read as compact spacecraft. Preserve straight edges while making noses, engine blocks and body proportions less conventional.
  • Pelican: our crossed rotor clearly says helicopter. The original manual specifies a transport plane. This is a design mismatch, not merely a shading preference.
  • Polar and heavy tanks: our Polar has useful slab skirts, but the original reference feels more massive and sharply contrasted. Several of our tank silhouettes span 28 visible pixels, which compresses their apparent size hierarchy.
  • Infantry: the manual specifies powered suits. Our small scale is right for the user's brief; stronger shoulders, armored boots and a compact stance could carry the original identity without enlarging the figure.
  • Added equipment: our Titan's missile pod and Slagger's hover chassis were remake inventions. They aid differentiation, but should not be mistaken for verified original designs.

Design identities checked against Konami's hosted original Military Madness manual, printed pages 8–9. Some US/Japanese names and descriptions differ; this review does not propose rule or stat changes.

Read the art at map scale

The original's compact icons belong to a dense, dark battlefield. Our larger pale production hexes create much more empty space around the new 32-pixel units. Judge their final presence again on the planned flattened terrain before changing sprite size.

Compared with our earlier remake

The earlier 22 × 18 source matrices used large dark frames, stronger block shapes and enlarged fractional rendering. Their broad silhouettes were forceful, but proportions, infantry scale and edge quality were less controlled. The new set fixes those problems. The strongest next pass would retain that discipline while recovering the original's heavier masses, clearer value groups and distinctive science-fiction machinery.
