The shared rulebook

All proposals use the existing rules: squads of 1–8, the same experience, ZOC, terrain defense, support, surround, factories and victory conditions. No new economy, damage type, healing, stealth or sensor rule is invented.

Logistics & compatibility

All can ride a Pelican and use factories. The stock Mule’s whitelist excludes these new IDs. Stationary units can exit a factory or unload onto legal terrain, then need transport to relocate. All proposals count for elimination, including in storage; the original Atlas/Trigger exceptions are ID-specific.

What is complete here?

Each has exact stats, turn rules, terrain behavior, a role, weaknesses and original directional faction artwork. The download contains engine-readable custom definitions; the gallery artwork is a separate pack. Numerical novelty is measured. Scenario balance and AI use remain untested.

Reproducible, bounded, explicit

How the 15 were selected

“Biggest gap” means greatest distance to the closest occupied configuration in a constrained candidate sample. It is not a proof of the largest hole in every imaginable ruleset.

  1. Read all 23 stock definitions; exclude the three aircraft and flying Pelican, leaving 19 non-air units.
  2. Draw 240,000 numerical configurations with fixed seed 19890922, before assigning names or equipment.
  3. Apply the feasibility limits below, merge behaviorally identical candidates, and reject direct upgrades or downgrades under a conservative capability comparison.
  4. Choose the candidate furthest from its nearest existing unit. Add it to the occupied set, update every distance, and repeat 15 times. Exclude direct upgrades/downgrades of already selected proposals too.
  5. Only then assign names, chassis silhouettes and tactical interpretations. No class quota or fictional equipment brief drives selection.

The distance function

Weighted distance, scaled to 0–100. Firepower is the mean absolute difference in ground/90 and air/85 attack. Armor is defense/80. Range compares the actual covered rings using Jaccard distance, averaged across domains.

Mobility combines normalized straight-line reach on road, plain, hill, waste, mountain and valley (75%) with passability (25%). This distinguishes a one-point tracked crawler from a road-bound one-point wheeled unit. Reach assumes uniform terrain and no ZOC.

The rules block averages capture permission, effective turn policy, passenger rights and transport-dependent placement. Passenger rights use 75% field access and 25% factory-only access. Turn policy collapses all immobile units to “stationary.” Class labels and names do not affect distance.

All units project ZOC, use the same combat model and carry the same squad/experience limits, so these constant rules add no distance. Map-dependent opportunity, opponent choices and actual campaign usage frequency are not modeled.

Candidate bounds, power screen & limitations

Attack: ground 0–90 by 10; air 0–80 by 10 plus 85. Defense 10–80 by 10; movement 0–9. Ground maximum range 0–6; air 0–5. A zero attack has zero range and vice versa. The grid samples combinations, not every possible integer.

Ground movement uses foot, wheels or treads. Foot is capped at move 5, defense 30 and combined attack 80, with no cargo. Wheels are capped at defense 40. Capture is capped at defense 30 and range 2, with no cargo. These are declared design assumptions, not restrictions imposed by the engine.

Retreat requires move ≥3, defense ≤40, and range ≤3. Ranges ≥4 require move-or-fire; ground range 6 is stationary. Stationary designs use tracked terrain legality. Unarmed candidates must carry cargo or be stationary with defense ≥40.

Cargo is 0 or 1. Carriers use ordinary move-then-fire, direct weapons ≤40 per domain, and one of three passenger policies: stock foot/bike infantry, Mule rules, or all ground units. A tracked candidate may inherit Giant’s waste restriction.

The screening sum is G/90 + A/85 + D/80 + M/9 + max(0, RG−1)/10 + max(0, RA−1)/8 + 0.3 capture + 0.3 cargo + 0.25 retreat − 0.25 mobile move-or-fire. It must not exceed Giant’s 2.69281. This is an explicit stacking limit, not a fitted value or proven balance budget.

A dominance comparison requires the same effective turn policy, no weaker attack or defense, no worse terrain reach/access, and supersets of firing rings, capture and passenger rights. This conservative screen catches obvious upgrades, not every tactical advantage.

Maximin favors unfamiliar combinations and boundaries; different bounds or weights change the answer. No transport survives the top-15 gap selection here. That is a result, not a claim that transport design is exhausted.

Sensitivity: increase each feature block’s weight by 50%
