everyone who worked on any version of Nectaris (design, programming, art, music,
production, localization and related work) by name, year, company and location;

## How this was compiled

- Five research passes on 2026-09-27, one per slice: the 1989 original and its
  US release; the PC ports, Windows freeware and Hudson's map contest; the
  1994–1999 console versions; the 2009 remake and re-releases; and the fan
  community whose documentation this remake uses. The raw notes stayed outside
  the repository; this file keeps the results and their source links. The same
  day, key credits were rechecked against their primary pages: the Neo Nectaris
  staff roll, Iwasaki's 2020 account of the 1989 team, the administrator line of
  the site this repository calls "Anka's", and the 1995 German version's
  publisher.
- Sources: in-game staff rolls (transcribed at [raido.moe](https://raido.moe/)),
  MobyGames credits (read through Wayback Machine copies where MobyGames refused
  automated access), manuals, company pages, Japanese Wikipedia, former staff's
  blogs and books, and each person's own site or profile.
- The 1989 HuCard and the 1990 TurboGrafx-16 release have no staff roll and no
  manual credits. Their team comes from later testimony: mainly Hiromasa
  Iwasaki's blog (2010–2024), Taiichi Matsuda's 2020 posts, June Chikuma's own
  works list, and the [ノンクレジット・レポート wiki](https://w.atwiki.jp/ncgamereport/pages/15.html),
  which records the staff of uncredited games.
- Privacy rules: only channels the person or their employer publishes; no home
  addresses, phone numbers, guessed addresses or people-search sites. An account
  is linked only when a bio, post, employer page or colleague's firsthand
  account ties it to the person's Hudson, studio or Nectaris work. Community
  members who use handles were not de-anonymized. Email addresses are not copied
  into this public file, even where published, because several owners obfuscate
  theirs against harvesting; the tables link to the page that shows the address
  or contact form.
- Confidence: **high** means the profile or page itself names Hudson, the
  studio or the game, or a colleague's firsthand account identifies it;
  **medium** means strong but indirect evidence, such as a distinctive name with
  a matching career; **low** or **unverified candidate** means a name match
  without linking evidence. Do not write to unverified candidates.

## How the game was made

Nectaris was the work of Isamu Izumi (和泉勇), a Hudson programmer who had just
ported R-TYPE to the PC Engine. He was its planner, designer and main
programmer, and his R-TYPE team built it at Hudson's head office in Sapporo
(the office is inferred from colleagues' accounts). The project began from his
liking for SystemSoft's Daisenryaku, and he kept Daisenryaku's hexes when a
colleague proposed a square grid
([Iwasaki 2010](http://www.highriskrevolution.com/wp/gamelife/2010/05/09/1989%E5%B9%B4-%E3%83%8F%E3%83%89%E3%82%BD%E3%83%B3%E3%81%A7%E4%BD%9C%E3%82%89%E3%82%8C%E3%81%A6%E3%81%84%E3%81%9F%E3%82%B2%E3%83%BC%E3%83%A0%E3%81%AB%E3%81%A4%E3%81%84%E3%81%A6/),
[Iwasaki 2020](http://www.highriskrevolution.com/wp/gamelife/2020/04/07/%E3%80%8E%E3%83%8D%E3%82%AF%E3%82%BF%E3%83%AA%E3%82%B9%E3%80%8F%E3%82%92%E3%82%81%E3%81%90%E3%82%8B%E6%80%9D%E3%81%84%E5%87%BA/)).

That colleague, Hiromasa Iwasaki, played board wargames. He lent Izumi Hobby
Japan's BASIC3 pack and explained its Crossing the Suez game. By Iwasaki's
account this is where Nectaris got its fixed armies with no unit production,
its strong zones of control, vehicles that can move after attacking, and the
halving of a target whose surrounding hexes are all covered by the attacker's
zones of control. Izumi turned down his proposal for a combined-arms bonus as
too complex. The account is Iwasaki's own and has no second source.

Taiichi Matsuda, who drew the units, posted the 1988 planning document in
April 2020. Its cover reads "War Simulation Game — LONG REINS", apparently the
working title. He also revealed the team's hidden backstory: the Gaichi Empire
stood for Hokkaido and the Union for Honshu, and the Gaichi leader "Modeler F.
Scratch" was modeled on Matsuda himself
([post](https://x.com/sentinel_sp/status/1247120840841261058),
[planning document](http://www.highriskrevolution.com/wp/gamelife/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2020/04/EU6smY8UEAgNjmT.jpg)).
In a 2020 Konami interview he called Nectaris about the only game he worked on
whose planning, programming and design were all done by Hudson staff
([Konami](https://www.konami.com/games/pcemini/topics/jp/ja/topics_8)).

### 1992 SystemSoft PC-9801 and X68000 ports

SystemSoft of Fukuoka, the maker of Daisenryaku, ported the HuCard game in
September 1992 ([8BITS](https://www.8-bits.info/gamelist/X68K/info/info_XjzR3wR47qbQTs5d.php),
[KrK](https://www.ne.jp/asahi/krk/kct/misc/nectaris.htm)). No staff list was
found. A reader comment on
[Iwasaki's 2020 post](http://www.highriskrevolution.com/wp/gamelife/2020/04/07/%E3%80%8E%E3%83%8D%E3%82%AF%E3%82%BF%E3%83%AA%E3%82%B9%E3%80%8F%E3%82%92%E3%82%81%E3%81%90%E3%82%8B%E6%80%9D%E3%81%84%E5%87%BA/)
recalls an X68000 port programmer, initials "F.K", who found that the
original's Hawkeye target selection reads a wrong work value at the end of its
calculation.

### 1997–1999 Windows freeware and the LOGiN map contest

Hudson's Windows versions name no individuals: every About box, dialog,
installer string and MIDI file in the archived Ver 97.11.5 installer credits
only "HUDSON" / 株式会社ハドソン (checked 2026-09-27). June Chikuma's works list
credits her, with Kenichi Nishimoto, for the Windows music. The 1997 map
contest ran through ASCII's LOGiN magazine using the Windows editor
([BASE NECTARIS](http://www.max.hi-ho.ne.jp/summoner/nectaris/history/windows/index.htm));
44 maps, including the winners', went into the PlayStation and Game Boy
versions, and the PlayStation roll names 16 winners (listed in the PlayStation
section below).

project. The short drafts fit an X direct message or a LinkedIn connection note;
